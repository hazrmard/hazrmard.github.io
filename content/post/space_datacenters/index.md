## The viability equation

Whether datacenters in space make sense:

$$
\sum_{part \in Parts}\left( Revenue - \frac{CapEx}{lifetime} - (OpEx + launch_cost)\right) \cdot (1 - failure_rate) > 0
$$

Where:

$$
Revenue \propto FLOPS
$$

$$
Power \propto Frequency \times Voltage^2 \times Capacitance
$$

subject to thermal design power.

$$
Thermal power = Power
$$

$$
FLOPS \propto Frequency \times Instructions Per Cycle (IPC)
$$

Characterizing each part: 

```
class Part:
    mass: float
    capex: float
    opex: float # ground support staffing, software etc
    revenue_rate: float # per FLOPS
    power: float # +ive for producing, -ive for consuming
    waste_heat_factor: float # [0,1]
    mttf: float
```


## Can't escape heat

In space, there is no air. The only means of expelling heat is radiation. Thermal radiators can be used to absorb heat from the computer, and then emit it into space. The fundamental conservation of energy must be conserved.

Heat in = heat out

Where, the heat into the radiator is the power generated by the computer, $P$.

### Calculating compute power

We model power consumption as a function of the hardware specification (max potential), and the compute workload (realized potential).

P = C V^2 f

### Radiating away heat

$$
P_{loss} = A \epsilon \sigma T^4
$$


## Chipping away

The big causes of chip degradation:

1. Bias Temperature Instability increases threshold voltage due to trapped charges,
2. Hot carrier injection due to high-speed electrons smashing into silicon lattice, reducing mobility,
3. Time dependent dielectric breakdown from continuous use, causing short circuit across the gate insulation,
4. Electromigration can break circuits or cause short circuits in the copper wiring.

$$
\text{Useful Life (MTTF)} \propto \frac{1}{f \cdot D} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{V}\right)^\gamma \cdot \exp\left(\frac{E_a}{k \cdot T(V, f, D)}\right)
$$

where $\gamma$ is voltage acceleration factor ~4-7, D is duty cycle, E_a is activation energy of the dominant feature, and T is a function of voltage, frequency, and duty cycle.
